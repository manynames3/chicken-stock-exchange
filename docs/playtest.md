# Human playtest still to perform

These sessions have not been performed. Automated browser checks and seeded simulations verify mechanics and regressions, but do not prove the game is fun or balanced for humans.

Use the deployed game with five first-time players, then at least two tables of three or four friends. Keep the tutorial optional. Record:

- Can a newcomer identify cash, share value, and total assets and place a valid order? After optional practice, can they explain a news/demand/dice equation and a protection payout without help?
- On a phone, can players choose a stock, quantity, and protection, and confirm the saved order comfortably? Check keyboard use, reduced motion, and reconnection on actual devices.
- Do reveal pacing and the eight-second recap feel clear or slow? Do players understand why they won or lost from the finale?
- Across repeat games, do concentration or perpetual-buy strategies dominate? Compare three-share and five-share rules in separate sessions, keeping news seeds and starting assets equivalent when possible.
- Does Easy let a beginner learn, Normal offer a fair challenge, and Hard reward deliberate play? Computer simulation win rates are comparisons against scripted policies, not target human win rates.

Keep a small session log: player experience, device, tutorial choice, CPU level or table size, match duration, confusing moments, winner strategy, and whether participants choose a rematch. Use recurring problems to select rule or UX changes; do not label the game fully balanced from the current automated probe alone.
